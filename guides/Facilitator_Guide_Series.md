# At a glance

Four lectures for 3rd-year MBBS (Community Medicine). Each runs 45 to 50 minutes. Each has an HTML deck, a Typst PDF deck, a quiz, and its own facilitator guide.

| Lecture | Title | Period | Guide |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ancient medicine: how people explained illness | Prehistory to about 500 AD | `Facilitator_Guide_L1` |
| 2 | The scientific revolution in medicine | About 500 AD to 1700 | `Facilitator_Guide_L2` |
| 3 | The birth of public health | 1700 to 1900 | `Facilitator_Guide_L3` |
| 4 | Public health in the twentieth century and now | 1900 to present | `Facilitator_Guide_L4` |

# The thread to repeat in every lecture

From explanation by authority, to explanation by observation, to action on populations, to the conditions that make people healthy. End each lecture by saying: **count, compare, act, check**.

# How to run a session

1. Open the HTML deck in a browser. Press `F` for full screen. Press `S` for speaker view. Press `?` for help.
2. Start with the question on the objectives slide. Let students guess before you answer.
3. Keep to the timing table in the lecture guide. Cut from the "short of time" list, not from the quiz.
4. Run the quiz last. Use hands up or a phone poll. Read the wrong-option notes in the guide before you explain.
5. Close with the take-home slide and the link to the next lecture.

# Rules for teaching history honestly

- Say how sure you are. Many ancient dates are approximate. Say "about".
- Separate the person from the legend. Check the "myths" section of each guide.
- Name what the old idea got right and wrong. Miasma was wrong on cause and right on action.
- Teach ethics episodes (Tuskegee, Nuremberg) with care. See the L4 guide.

# Suggested assessment

- Use the quiz in each deck as a formative check.
- For internal assessment, turn two quiz items per lecture into short answers, for example "Explain why Snow's method mattered although he did not know the cause."
- Ask one reflective question after Lecture 4: "Which determinant of health in your own clinic area would you act on first, and why?"
