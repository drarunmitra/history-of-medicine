# Gap-fill facts (written 2026-11-15)

Shared source of facts for the new slides (Quarto set and Beamer PDFs).
Written from memory of standard sources. Items marked **[verify]** must be checked before teaching. Do not drop the marker.

## Ancient (add to L1 / Part 1)
- **China.** *Huangdi Neijing* (Yellow Emperor's Inner Classic): compiled in the Han period, about 2nd to 1st century BC; the Yellow Emperor attribution is traditional. Ideas: yin-yang, five phases, qi, channels (acupuncture). Prevention theme: the best physician treats a disturbance before it becomes disease (Suwen ch. 2). Variolation (inoculation against smallpox) is recorded in China from about the 16th century [verify].
- **Egypt.** Imhotep: vizier and architect of the Step Pyramid at Saqqara (about 2650 BC), later deified as a healer. No medical text is securely his [verify].
- **Rome.** Cloaca Maxima (traditionally 6th century BC). First aqueduct Aqua Appia, 312 BC. Frontinus, water commissioner, *De aquaeductu* about AD 97, lists nine aqueducts [verify]. Vitruvius (1st century BC) advised siting towns away from marshes. Military hospitals (valetudinaria). Roman lesson: public health as engineering and state works.
- **Alexandria.** Herophilus (about 335 to 280 BC) and Erasistratus (about 304 to 250 BC) dissected human bodies under the Ptolemies. Herophilus: brain as seat of intelligence, nerves, pulse. Later sources (Celsus) say condemned prisoners were vivisected; this is disputed [verify].
- **India.** Charaka Samhita: the main text of Ayurvedic internal medicine; compiled over centuries, about 1st century BC to 2nd century AD [verify]. Sushruta is already in L1.

## Renaissance (add to L2 / Part 2)
- **Fracastoro** (1478 to 1553), Verona. *Syphilis sive morbus Gallicus* (poem, 1530) gave syphilis its name. *De contagione* (1546): "seminaria" (seeds) of contagion; spread by direct contact, by fomites (objects), and at a distance. An early contagion theory, 300 years before germ theory.

## 19th century (add to L3 / Part 2)
- **Semmelweis**, Vienna General Hospital, 1847. First clinic (doctors and students, who also did autopsies) had maternal mortality about 10%; second clinic (midwives) about 4% [verify exact figures]. After chlorinated-lime handwashing from May 1847 the first clinic fell to about 1 to 2% [verify]. He published in 1861; his idea was rejected; died 1865 in an asylum. Lesson: right result, wrong persuasion, no mechanism yet (germ theory came later).
- **Lister.** Carbolic acid antisepsis; first case 1865, published in the Lancet 1867; Glasgow. Inspired by Pasteur's work on microbes.
- **Nightingale.** Crimean War 1854 to 56, Scutari. Death rate in the hospital about 42% in Feb 1855, about 2% by June 1855 after the Sanitary Commission (arrived March 1855) fixed drains and ventilation [verify; her own figures]. Polar-area diagram 1858. First woman Fellow of the Royal Statistical Society (1858). *Notes on Nursing* 1859. Later worked on sanitation and health of the army in India (Royal Commission 1859) [verify]. Lesson: data plus a picture that persuades officials.
- **Virchow**, 1848 report on a typhus epidemic in Upper Silesia: causes were social and political; "medicine is a social science" (attributed phrase).
- **Ross.** 20 Aug 1897, in Secunderabad (Hyderabad), found malaria parasites in a mosquito's stomach; Nobel Prize 1902. 1911 mathematical model of malaria (Ross). Kermack and McKendrick 1927 (SIR model).
- **India, plague.** Bombay plague 1896. Haffkine's plague vaccine 1897. Epidemic Diseases Act 1897 (still used in 2020 for COVID-19). [verify details]

## 20th century (new L4 / Part 2 end)
- **Flexner Report**, 1910 (Carnegie Foundation, Bulletin No. 4). Made medical education science based, laboratory and hospital centred; many schools closed. Critics: it narrowed attention to the individual patient and weakened social and population thinking; it hurt Black and women's colleges.
- **Penicillin.** Fleming noticed the mould effect in 1928. Florey and Chain developed it; Nobel Prize 1945. First treated patient Albert Alexander, Oxford, 1941 [verify]. Antibiotic era leads to the antimicrobial resistance problem.
- **WHO** constitution in force 7 April 1948. Health defined as "a state of complete physical, mental and social well-being and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity".
- **NHS**, UK, started 5 July 1948. Beveridge Report 1942.
- **Epidemiology.** Framingham Heart Study began 1948. Doll and Hill: 1950 case-control study of smoking and lung cancer; 1954 report from the British doctors cohort. Hill's criteria 1965.
- **Bhore Committee** 1946: already in L3.
- **Smallpox eradication.** Intensified programme from 1967; last natural case Somalia, October 1977; WHO declared eradication on 8 May 1980. Already in L3.
- **Omran 1971**, epidemiologic transition: age of pestilence and famine, age of receding pandemics, age of degenerative and man-made diseases.
- **Lalonde Report**, Canada 1974: health field concept with four parts: human biology, environment, lifestyle, health care organisation.
- **Alma-Ata** Declaration, 6 to 12 September 1978: primary health care, "Health for All". Selective PHC (Walsh and Warren, 1979) as a narrower alternative. Astana Declaration 2018.
- **Ottawa Charter**, November 1986: health promotion. Five actions: build healthy public policy, create supportive environments, strengthen community action, develop personal skills, reorient health services.
- **Social determinants.** Black Report (UK, 1980); Whitehall studies (Marmot); WHO Commission on Social Determinants of Health, 2008: "Closing the gap in a generation".

## Ethics
- **Nuremberg Code**, 1947 (from the Doctors' Trial): voluntary consent is essential.
- **Tuskegee** syphilis study, 1932 to 1972, untreated Black men, stopped after press exposure in 1972.
- **Declaration of Helsinki**, World Medical Association, 1964 (revised many times).
- **Belmont Report**, 1979: respect for persons, beneficence, justice.
- **India:** ICMR ethical guidelines (first 1980; latest 2017) [verify].

## India, modern
- Polio: India last wild case 13 Jan 2011; WHO South-East Asia Region certified polio-free 27 March 2014.
- National Rural Health Mission 2005 (later NHM). Ayushman Bharat announced 2018: Health and Wellness Centres plus PM-JAY insurance (launched Sept 2018) [verify].

## Recent (L4 / Part 2 end)
- **COVID-19.** WHO declared a public health emergency of international concern on 30 Jan 2020 and a pandemic on 11 March 2020. WHO estimate of excess deaths 2020 to 2021: about 14.9 million [verify]. First vaccines authorised within about a year. WHO Pandemic Agreement adopted May 2025 [verify].
- **Antimicrobial resistance.** GRAM study (Lancet 2022): about 1.27 million deaths directly attributable to bacterial AMR in 2019; about 4.95 million associated [verify].
- **One Health.** Quadripartite alliance (FAO, WOAH, UNEP, WHO), 2022.
